# Judgment and Beauty

This is a website we made for our HTML and CSS group project, based on *Judgment and Beauty* by Yoon Soo Lee.

[Visit our website](https://jasmine-chang77.github.io/spread/) · [Read the original article](https://liminalities.net/12-2/judgment.pdf)

## About the reading

Lee writes about how judgment affects the way we create and see beauty. She questions how much we rely on other people's opinions and reflects on learning to trust ourselves, make mistakes, and approach our work with more love.

What stood out to us was the tension between wanting to express ourselves and worrying about how others will respond. We used that idea as the starting point for our design.

## Our design

### Judgment as a frame

The top navigation and bottom bar form a frame around the page. We wanted this to suggest the limits that judgment can place on us. The navigation also lets readers jump between the five sections of the article without having to scroll through everything.

We carried the same idea into the body by adding borders to both sides of the blockquotes.

### Colors and fonts

The red and orange come from Lee's *Practice Love* paintings in the original article. We paired them with a warm cream background to keep that connection throughout the website.

We used **Sentient** for the body text and **Albert Sans** for headings and larger statements. Sentient gives the longer passages a softer feel, while Albert Sans makes the more expressive parts stand out. The large quotation marks use **Kalnia**.

### Warning tags

Around the title, we placed questions such as “Is it beautiful?” and “Is it cliché?” These come from the author's doubts about her own work. We made them look like warning stamps to introduce that feeling of being judged before the reader starts the article.

### Quotes and highlights

Some passages felt like they needed a different pace. For one quote, we left more space around the text and added large quotation marks so the words would feel as though they were floating in the air. We wanted readers to stop for a moment and think about them.

We also used the HTML `<mark>` element to highlight a passage about allowing students to fail. To us, this connects closely to the reading's message about trusting our own judgment and learning through experience.

Links use colors from the same palette, with underlines and hover effects to make them easy to recognize.

### The ending

We ended with four words in large type: **Judgment. Beauty. Practice. Love.** They bring together the ideas we kept coming back to while reading and designing the page.

## Built with

- HTML for the article structure, section links, quotes, and author dialog
- CSS for the layout, typography, colors, and hover effects
- Media queries to adapt the page to different screen sizes and print
- GitHub Pages to publish the website
